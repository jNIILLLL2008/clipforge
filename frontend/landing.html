How it works

Three decisions, then it runs itself.

Everything after setup is one button — or nothing at all, if you leave the daily schedule on.

01

Pick a format

Seven presets, each a different shape of video rather than a topic — countdown, fast meme cut, calm loop, one specific TV show. Shape is what holds attention. Then change any of the 68 settings behind it.

02

Point it at footage

Upload your own clips, or pull from stock libraries licensed for commercial reuse. Every clip carries its licence through the pipeline and into the description.

03

Press Publish

It sources, cuts, scores, renders, writes the metadata and uploads. Connect a channel once and finished videos land there on their own, private until you say otherwise.

The retention gate

It will refuse to make a bad video.

Every video is scored before a single frame is encoded. Below 55 the run is rejected, you are told exactly which rule failed, and the run is refunded to your monthly allowance.

Opening clip reaches the action inside the hook window 25
No single shot runs past its limit 20
Cuts per minute land in a watchable band 20
Something promises a payoff — a countdown or a list 20
On-screen text, because most short-form is watched muted 10
Total length is sane for the format 5
93.3 passed — flagged “only 5.3 cuts a minute; the edit feels static”

A rejection is the tool working.

Most tools in this category will happily render anything you point them at, and you find out it was weak after it has been sitting on your channel for a week.

Scoring the plan instead of the finished file means a weak video costs nothing — no encode, no upload, no allowance spent. And because each rule is specific, the failure tells you what to change: cut faster, shorten the opening, turn the numbered list on.

Videos that pass still report what was weakest, so the next one is better.

Footage & licensing

Every clip carries its licence.

Sources declare whether their footage may be reused commercially, and the pipeline re-checks it on every single clip — not just once per source.

Your own uploads

Drag clips in. You hold the rights, so there is nothing to claim. The only way to cover sport, gaming or anything from a broadcast.

Licensed stock

Pexels and Pixabay, both licensed for commercial use without attribution.

Open collections

Openverse and the Internet Archive — CC0, public domain, CC BY and BY-SA only. Non-commercial and no-derivatives licences are refused, and required credit is written into the description for you.

What this does not do. No amount of engineering makes someone else’s broadcast footage safe to re-upload — Content ID matches the content itself, whoever posted it. If your niche is a TV show, the honest route is footage you have the rights to, through the upload source.

Self-hosting? There is an adapter for pulling from YouTube. It ships disabled and takes two deliberate opt-ins to switch on, because the terms-of-service and liability trade-off is a decision, not a default.

Questions

Before you sign up

Do I need my own footage?

No — the stock and open-collection sources work on their own. But they carry no broadcast, sport or gaming footage, so if that is your niche you will need to supply clips you have the rights to.

Will my videos get copyright claims?

Not from footage this tool sourced for you: everything it uses is either yours or licensed for commercial reuse, and non-commercial licences are refused outright. If you upload clips you do not own, that is a different matter and no tool can help with it.

What does “daily automation” actually do?

Pick a time and a timezone. Our servers queue a run when that time arrives, so nothing needs to stay open on your machine. An account runs at most once per calendar day, and a window missed by more than two hours is skipped rather than fired late at night.

Can I see a video before it goes out?

Yes. Use Dry run to render without uploading, or leave visibility on private — which is the default for every new account — and publish from YouTube once you have watched it.

What happens if it cannot find enough clips?

The run fails with a plain explanation rather than shipping a two-clip video, and the run is refunded to your allowance.
