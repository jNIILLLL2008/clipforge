Footage and licensing

Your footage, and nothing else.

ClipForge does not go and find clips for you. Every run starts from footage you uploaded, which is the only version of this that is honestly safe to sell.

You hold the rights

Drag your clips in and there is nothing to claim, because nothing came from anyone else. It is also the only way to cover sport, gaming or anything from a broadcast.

No stock libraries

Stock and public-domain libraries hold generic clips. Nothing broadcast, nothing sport, nothing gaming. For what actually gets made here they were dead weight, so the pipeline does not carry them at all.

What this does not do

Uploading is not a loophole. If you upload someone else’s broadcast footage, Content ID still matches the content itself, whoever posted it, and that is between you and the rights holder. The tool makes no claim it can help there.

Self-hosting? There is an adapter for pulling from YouTube. It ships disabled and takes two deliberate opt-ins to switch on, because the terms-of-service and liability trade-off is a decision, not a default.

Before you sign up

Do I need my own footage?

Yes. ClipForge cuts footage you upload and does not source any on your behalf, so bring clips you have the rights to. Stock and public-domain sources were removed because they hold nothing useful for broadcast, sport or gaming.

Will my videos get copyright claims?

Nothing is sourced for you, so no claim can originate with the tool. Every frame it touches is one you uploaded. If you upload clips you do not own, that is a different matter, and no tool can help with it.

What does daily automation actually do?

Pick a time and a timezone. Our servers queue a run when that time arrives, so nothing needs to stay open on your machine. An account runs at most once per calendar day, and a window missed by more than two hours is skipped rather than fired late at night.

Can I see a video before it goes out?

Yes. Use Dry run to render without uploading, or leave visibility on private, which is the default for every new account, and publish from YouTube once you have watched it.

What happens if it cannot find enough clips?

The run fails with a plain explanation rather than shipping a two-clip video, and the run is refunded to your allowance.
